# Design Principles: What to Look For

| Principle | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual hierarchy** | One clear focal point; size and weight follow importance (title → key details → supporting info → fine print) | Everything competes; the date is smaller than decoration |
| **Alignment** | Elements share a few strong edges (e.g. one left margin) | Mixed centered and left-aligned text with no reason; items "floating" |
| **Proximity / grouping** | Related information sits together; groups are separated by clear space | Date far from location; label far from its button |
| **Contrast** | Text clearly stands out from its background | Light yellow or light grey text on white |
| **White space** | Generous, consistent margins; breathing room around groups | Crowded edges; text touching the canvas edge |
| **Repetition / consistency** | Same style for same role (all bullets, all body text) | A new font or color for every line |
| **Typography** | 1–2 families; readable sizes; sensible line length | 4+ fonts; all-caps paragraphs; tiny body text |
| **Color** | Limited palette (3–5 colors) with a clear accent for the call to action | Many unrelated saturated colors |
| **Call to action** | Obvious, button-like, high contrast, near the end of the reading path | Small plain text in a corner |

## Accessibility: measurable checks

**Color contrast (WCAG 2.x):**

| Text | Minimum (AA) | Enhanced (AAA) |
|---|---|---|
| Normal text | 4.5 : 1 | 7 : 1 |
| Large text (≥ 24 px regular or ≥ 18.66 px bold) | 3 : 1 | 4.5 : 1 |

Contrast ratio = (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), where L1 and L2 are the relative luminances of the lighter and darker colors.

**Other checks:**
- Body text at least ~16 px at the intended display size (larger for posters seen from a distance)
- Information is not conveyed by color alone
- Text over photos has a solid or darkened backing

## Bilingual layouts (EN/ES)
- Spanish text runs about 15–30% longer than English; leave room
- Keep accents and inverted punctuation (¿ ¡) intact
- Give both languages a clear, consistent hierarchy (e.g. Spanish as subtitle in a lighter weight)
